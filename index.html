What I refit

Almost every small business runs the same loop: something happens on a job, it gets written up, and it goes out to a customer. The writing up and the going out is where the evenings go. That is what I rebuild, in the order that costs you most.

Marketing

Every finished job turned into a post, a case study and a review request in fifteen minutes. A month of posts written in one sitting, in your voice.

Operations

Site visits, consultations and job notes spoken into your phone and turned into briefs, job sheets, quotes and schedules the same day.

Customers

Same-day replies to enquiries. Follow-up that happens. A spring email to every past client. Reviews asked for at the right moment.

Billing

Proposals with staged fees and a validity date. Every quote chased on day five and day twelve. Ten minutes every Friday, and nothing left unquoted or unchased.

Questions people ask

The honest answers, because they are the ones you will ask on the call anyway.

Will this replace me?
No. It cannot visit the site, win the trust or make the call. It types, you decide. Every workflow I build has your sign-off designed into it as a step, not a hope.
I tried ChatGPT and it was rubbish.
Almost certainly because it knew nothing about your business. An assistant with your prices, your past proposals and your tone of voice attached is a different tool from a blank chat box. That set-up is most of the job.
It makes things up.
It does, and predictably: prices, regulations, plant names, anything it was not given. So the assistants are only allowed to use your attached files for facts, they flag anything uncertain, and you read before anything goes out. Catching a mistake is the system working.
Where do my clients' details go?
We turn off training on your data in the account settings together, keep client files in your own storage rather than in the tool, and use first names or initials for drafting. If your trade touches health, money or children, the rules get stricter and I point you at the ICO's small-business guidance.
I'm not technical.
Good. Your first ten uses are copy and paste from a printed card. Everything runs on the phone in the van as well as at a desk. If it needs a manual, I have built it wrong.
What happens when the tools change?
They will. The playbook is yours to edit, the prompts are plain English, and nothing depends on a feature that only exists this month. If a change breaks something before the check-in, I fix it.
